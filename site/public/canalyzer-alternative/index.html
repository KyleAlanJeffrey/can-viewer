A free CANalyzer alternative for log analysis

If you need to read CAN logs, decode them with a DBC and plot signals, FreeCAN Studio does that in a browser tab, for free. It opens Vector BLF and ASC logs, MF4 bus logging files, PEAK TRC traces, CSV exports and candump logs.

Open FreeCAN Studio Try the Demo →

Free. No account. In the app, choose Open Log… or drop the log file on the window.

Files are processed on your computer and never uploaded.

How to analyse a CAN log in FreeCAN Studio

  1. Open the app

    Go to app.freecanstudio.com in a desktop browser. There is nothing to install and no account to create.

  2. Open the log

    Click Open Log… and choose a BLF, ASC or other log, or drop it anywhere on the window. It is read on your computer, and the format is recognized from the file.

  3. Decode and explore

    Click Open DBC… to add the database for your bus. See every ID in Overview, read frames in Trace and plot signals in Plot.

What it does, and what it doesn’t

FreeCAN Studio works on recorded logs, and can try recording a bus itself through a low-cost USB adapter. Here is where it fits.

  • It does

    Open BLF, ASC, MF4, TRC, CSV and candump logs. Decode them with one or more DBC files, including J1939 by PGN. Plot signals with cursors. Show bit activity to reverse-engineer unknown messages. Edit and export DBC files. In Chrome or Edge, capture a bus live from slcan adapters such as CANable or USBtin, or candleLight (gs_usb) adapters, and save the capture as a log; live capture is experimental and not yet tested with real hardware. All of it on your computer, in a browser tab.

  • It doesn’t

    Connect to Vector, PEAK or Kvaser interfaces, or capture in Firefox or Safari. It never sends frames, so there is no transmit or replay. It has no scripting, and it reads CAN only, not LIN, FlexRay or Ethernet.

Questions

Are my logs uploaded?

No. FreeCAN Studio runs in your browser: a Rust core compiled to WebAssembly reads the file on your computer. The app loads nothing from third parties, and its Content-Security-Policy makes your browser enforce that. How to check it.

Can FreeCAN Studio replace CANalyzer?

For reading and decoding recorded logs it may be all you need, and it can record a bus with an slcan or candleLight adapter, though live capture is still experimental. It does not work with Vector, PEAK or Kvaser interfaces, it cannot transmit or replay traffic, and it has no scripting. If you need those, use a tool made for your hardware.

Can it record from a CAN adapter?

Yes, in Chrome or Edge on a desktop computer: plug in an slcan adapter, such as a CANable or USBtin, or a candleLight (gs_usb) adapter, and Overview, Trace and Plot follow the bus as it records. It only receives, and Save Capture… writes the recording as a candump log. Live capture is experimental and not yet tested with real hardware. More about live capture.

Which files from my existing setup can I open?

BLF and ASC logs and DBC databases, as well as MF4, PEAK TRC, CSV and candump logs. FreeCAN Studio reads the CAN frames in a log; LIN, FlexRay and Ethernet traffic is skipped. Other file types, such as tool configurations, are not read.

Is FreeCAN Studio made by Vector?

No. FreeCAN Studio is independent, and is not affiliated with, sponsored by or endorsed by Vector Informatik GmbH.

Which browsers does it run in?

Current desktop versions of Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari. Phones and tablets are not supported. Live capture from a USB CAN adapter is experimental and needs Chrome or Edge on a desktop computer.

Is it free?

Yes. FreeCAN Studio is free and needs no account.

Can I try it without a log?

Yes. Try the Demo opens a 1,000,000-frame log with its DBC.
